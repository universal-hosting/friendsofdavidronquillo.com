The Trial of Trust: A Reflection on 2006

Welcome visitor! This website provides factual clarity regarding the incident involving David Ronquillo that involved potential wrongdoing.

In the legal community of San Diego, my name was once synonymous with the "impossible" victory. By 2006, I had tried more than 100 jury trials, earning a reputation as one of the region’s premier trial attorneys. My firm held the AV Preeminent rating from Martindale-Hubbell - the absolute pinnacle of professional excellence and ethical standing. Moreover, I was elected to the Hall of Fame of my law school, the Thomas Jefferson School of Law in San Diego. I did not achieve this through mere academic skill; I believe the Lord gave me a phenomenal gift for the Art of Effective Communication, allowing me to win multi-million dollar results in cases other attorneys had rejected as unwinnable. For fifteen years leading up to this incident, by God’s grace, I was virtually unbeatable.

It was this very success - this "miraculous" track record - that drew my partner to me. He joined my firm as a partner and Chief Financial Benefactor specifically because of the favor he saw resting upon my work. As the Managing Partner and CFO, I was the engine of the firm, responsible for the livelihoods of my two partners and our six dedicated employees.

However, even at the height of professional triumph, I found myself facing a substantial financial crisis. The solution was already in hand: we had resolved a massive case with a recovery valued at five times our total debt. But as the debt came due immediately, the settlement funds were caught in a brief administrative delay.

In a moment of intense vulnerability and pressure to protect my staff and the firm’s future, I turned to the man who had invested in my success. My partner proposed a temporary bridge: utilize funds from the Client Trust Account until the settlement arrived. He gave me his word that he would personally cover the sum should any further delay occur. I was fully cognizant of the gravity and the risks of this decision. Despite the pressure and the assurances of my partner, the responsibility was mine alone. I chose to trust a man I believed was my ally to save the mission we had built together.

What followed was not a business failure, but a calculated dismantling of my life. Within forty-eight hours, that same partner abruptly terminated his position. I soon discovered a devastating reality: the settlement check, which should have restored the accounts, had been intentionally intercepted. My former partner had joined forces with the other attorney on that settlement to seize the entirety of the recovery.

Having orchestrated the delay that left the trust account empty, my former partner then used the very "solution" he had encouraged me to take as a weapon. He contacted my clients, systematically poisoning my reputation and convincing them to move their cases to his newly formed firm.

The self-termination by the former partner, and his nullification of the loan and guarantee without warning or notification that led to these tragic and wholly avoidable circumstances, was due to his sudden objection to my tithing on incoming funds, which I was fully authorized to do. His objection resulted from a profound personal tragedy he had experienced forty years prior in which he lost his son to suicide, and for which he blamed God. He subsequently came to take extreme offense to actions that supported God or His commandments, and thus I became a target for his wrath and retribution. Though many years had passed, the enduring weight of his unresolved grief resulted in a long-standing and intense personal struggle with unfortunate consequences for some of his associates.

I lost my practice, my career, my license to practice law, and my standing. While I was the victim of a sophisticated betrayal by those who were drawn to my light only to eventually extinguish it, I have spent twenty years taking full ownership of my error in judgment. I allowed the desperate need to protect my people to lead me into a trap set by the very person I trusted most. I carry the weight of that choice every day, leaning on the same faith that once guided me in the courtroom to find peace in this season of accountability.
